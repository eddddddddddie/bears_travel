Travel cost

Cost of travel by distance

Each match is modelled from the home side's view, controlling for team strength (Elo), rest and recent travel, with a separate home advantage for each season. Points are margin for the travelling team, relative to playing within 150 km of home. Bars are 95% intervals.

Margin change by trip distance, 1996 to 2026

Trips of 150 to 1,000 km reduce the travelling team's margin, and the interval excludes zero. The estimates for 1,000 to 3,000 km are smaller and their intervals include zero. Trips of 3,000 km and over have the largest estimated cost, with a wide interval because few matches involve them.

Home advantage

Home advantage and its travel component

The raw home margin is the designated home side's average margin. The home-ground part is the season intercept from the model: the home advantage remaining after travel, rest and team strength are accounted for. The difference between the two lines is the part attributable to the visiting team's travel.

Perth Bears 2027 outlook

The projection assumes a balanced draw: each club plays every other club equally often, and each away game is played at the opponent's base. Under that assumption a club's travel cost in away games equals its gain when the same opponents travel to it, so the projected season total is zero for every club. History

Western Reds, 1995 to 1997

The Western Reds are the only previous Perth club in the competition. Results are shown as margin against the Elo expectation, which includes the average home advantage, so zero means the Reds performed as an average club would have in the same fixture.

Western Reds against expectation

Away from Perth the Reds' margins were below expectation in all three seasons. At home their margins were close to expectation, so visiting teams did not show an equivalent travel cost in Perth. This is consistent with eastward travel costing more than westward travel, but each bar covers one season of home or away matches, so the difference is not statistically reliable.

Method

Method and limits

Results for 1995 to 2026 come from Rugby League Project, and closing lines from AusSportsBetting. Travel distance is measured from each club's base to the venue, and time-zone offsets are taken at kick-off, including daylight saving. The model uses OLS with robust standard errors on — matches. The market comparison uses — matches with a closing line.

  • Most trips over 3,000 km are between North Queensland and Auckland, or to Darwin. Few involve Perth, so the Bears' figure assumes a Perth trip costs the same as any other trip of that length.
  • The effect of a time-zone change cannot be estimated separately from distance, because nearly every time-zone crossing is a trip to or from New Zealand or Perth.
  • The 2027 projection uses a balanced draw. Neutral venues, Magic Round and any Bears home games outside Perth are not included.
  • Win chance converts points to probability for an evenly matched game, using the standard deviation of the model's residuals.
